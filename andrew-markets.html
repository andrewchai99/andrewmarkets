The mechanic

One position, two directions

A CFD is an agreement to exchange the difference in an instrument's price between open and close — no ownership of the underlying asset changes hands, which is what lets you profit whichever way the market moves.

▲ Go long

Buy if you expect the price to rise

Example: buy 10 CFDs on EUR/USD at 1.0850. Price rises to 1.0900 — a 50 pip move in your favour.

50 pips × 10 contracts × $10 /pip
= +$500.00 profit
▼ Go short

Sell if you expect the price to fall

Example: sell 10 CFDs on EUR/USD at 1.0850. Price falls to 1.0800 — a 50 pip move in your favour.

50 pips × 10 contracts × $10 /pip
= +$500.00 profit

Risk warning

Understand the risk before you trade

CFDs are complex instruments and come with a high risk of losing money rapidly due to leverage. 76.4% of retail investor accounts lose money when trading CFDs with this provider. You should consider whether you understand how CFDs work, and whether you can afford to take the high risk of losing your money.

Leverage can work against you as well as for you: losses can exceed your deposits on non-negative-balance-protected products, and the value of your positions can fall as well as rise. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results.
